The practice

The work is held together by one recurring idea: that good design acts as a catalyst. Roads are treated as public realm rather than conduits, green networks as social infrastructure rather than decoration, and the line between public and private as something to blur rather than defend.

It moves between the intimate and the civic — courtyards and pavilions that frame everyday encounters, corridors that stitch a neighbourhood back together, junctions that reclaim asphalt for people. Grounded in ecological thinking and real community life, it designs for what happens between buildings as much as inside them.
