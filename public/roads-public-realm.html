4. ROADS NOT AS CONDUITS,
BUT AS PUBLIC REALM _

Right-of-way study across five street types, East Hastings / Commercial Drive
Urban design studio, 2025 // w. [collaborator]

Roads take the largest share of public land in the block and are managed as though movement were their only purpose. The study re-reads each right of way as public space — surface, edge and frontage — and asks what it can hold besides traffic.

Each intersection geometry was examined and its turning radius rectified. Reducing the kerb radius from 12.0 to 4.5 metres shortens the crossing, forces the turn to be taken slowly, and returns the corner to the footpath rather than to the sweep of a truck.

Continuous bike lanes run the length of R2 and R3, so that a cyclist is never returned to mixed traffic mid-route. Removing surface parking removes the asphalt that drives the local heat island, and the width it releases is planted rather than paved.

Within the hierarchy only R1 and R2 carry through traffic. R3 becomes shared frontage for commerce and seating; R4 and R5 are given over to planting, play and informal use, and remain accessible to service vehicles at walking pace.

Corner geometry · kerb radius rectified at every intersection in the study area. The crossing shortens by 6.4 m and the footpath recovers the corner.

Right-of-way allocation · the carriageway gives up 6.0 m and the surface parking gives up 5.0 m. Nothing is widened; the same 20 metres is redistributed.
