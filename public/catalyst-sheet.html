01: Catalyst

Collective Housing

[Term, Instructor]
[Course code]

Catalyst is a mid-rise housing block on the edge of the Arbutus Greenway. The brief asked for density. The project asks whether density has to mean sameness, and answers by distributing unit types according to what each position in the block can actually offer.

Studios and one-bedrooms carry roughly sixty per cent of the units and sit deepest in the massing. The larger units take the ground the smaller ones cannot use: three-bedrooms hold the corner plots, where the outlook opens to a hundred and eighty degrees, and two-bedrooms face the greenway with nothing in front of them. Commercial space wraps the ground floor behind a continuous window, so the street stays occupied after the residents have gone up.

Circulation does the social work. Two single-loaded corridors open onto the external courtyard and one double-loaded corridor runs through the centre facing the internal one, so the walk to a front door passes other people rather than only other doors.

Green space is treated as a set of nodes rather than as the margin left over once the building is set out. Each node sits where movement already concentrates — at a corridor end, a courtyard edge, a change of level — so that the planting is encountered on the way to somewhere rather than visited deliberately. The two courtyards work as a pair: one external and open to the greenway, one internal and held by the block. Between them they give every unit a view onto shared ground.
